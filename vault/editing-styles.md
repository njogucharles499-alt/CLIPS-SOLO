# Editing Styles

Every clip is cut in exactly one of the five styles below. The style is chosen by `style-selection-rules.md` and controls pacing, music, captions, and which sections of the 80-point framework (`editing-framework.md`) are **critical** (must score ≥ 4/5).

All styles still pass the 20 technical rules in `editing-rules.md`. Where a style pushes against a rule, the rule wins.

---

## At a Glance

| Style          | Avg. shot length | Cuts / 10 s | Ideal duration | Music energy | Primary emotion      | Best arc       |
|----------------|------------------|-------------|----------------|--------------|----------------------|----------------|
| Fast/Energy    | 1–2 s            | 5–8         | 20–45 s        | High (120–150 BPM) | Excitement, urgency | Insight Bomb   |
| Cinematic      | 4–8 s            | 1–2         | 60–120 s       | Low → swell (60–90 BPM) | Awe, empathy   | Fall & Rise    |
| Comedy         | Varies by beat   | 2–4         | 15–45 s        | Minimal / none | Laughter            | Stakes Climb (comic) |
| Educational    | 3–5 s            | 2–3         | 45–90 s        | Low, neutral (90–110 BPM) | Curiosity, clarity | Insight Bomb |
| Inspirational  | 4–6 s → 2–3 s    | 1–2 → 3–5   | 45–90 s        | Builds to peak (80–120 BPM) | Hope, determination | Fall & Rise |

## Style 1 — Fast/Energy

**Use when:** the speaker is high-energy, the content is a list, hot take, rapid-fire tips, reaction, or bold claim, and the target platform is TikTok / Reels / Shorts.

**Critical framework sections:** E. Retention · F. Pacing · G. Rhythm · H. Cutting

**Rules:**
1. **Open mid-sentence.** Frame 1 is already at full energy — no breath, no greeting.
2. **Shot length 1–2 s.** Change something (angle, punch-in, B-roll, text pop) every 1–2 seconds.
3. **Respect the jump-cut ceiling.** Max 5 jump cuts in any 10 s (Rule 12); hit 5–8 total cuts by mixing in B-roll, punch-ins, and text, not more jump cuts.
4. **Cut every breath and filler.** Zero-gap dialogue, but keep the speaker sounding human (P33).
5. **Music 120–150 BPM**, cut on the beat; music ducks 12 dB+ under voice (Rule 11).
6. **Punch-ins on stressed words** — 110–120% scale, max one per key point (P39).
7. **Animated word-by-word captions**, bold, 1–3 words per block, keyword colour pop.
8. **SFX allowed** for emphasis (whoosh on transitions, hit on key words) — every one motivated (P63).
9. **Re-hook every 5–7 s** instead of every 7–10 s.
10. **End hard.** Last line then cut — no fade, no outro. Aim for a loopable seam (P24).

**Don't:** speed up talking-head footage (Rule 18), use template transitions unless the brand brief calls for a stylised look (Rule 17), or let energy dip in the middle third.

## Style 2 — Cinematic

**Use when:** the story is emotional, personal, or high-stakes; the visuals are strong (good lighting, B-roll, locations); the speaker is reflective; the platform tolerates longer watch time (YouTube, Instagram, LinkedIn).

**Critical framework sections:** B. Story · I. Emotion · J. Tension · N. Visual Composition

**Rules:**
1. **Let shots breathe.** 4–8 s average shot length; hold on faces through emotional beats (P42).
2. **Slow build.** Start quiet — ambient sound or a single line — and build intensity over the runtime.
3. **Keep the human moments.** Pauses, sighs, and voice cracks stay in (P43). Dramatic pauses held up to 2.5 s (Rule 4); longer silences are scored with ambience.
4. **J- and L-cuts everywhere.** Audio leads into scenes; picture lingers after lines end (P38).
5. **Score for emotion.** Sparse piano, strings, or ambient pads at 60–90 BPM, swelling only at the arc's turning point (P45 — never before it's earned).
6. **Slow-motion only on B-roll**, never on the speaker talking (Rule 18).
7. **Grade with intent.** Consistent, filmic grade; accurate skin tones (Rule 13).
8. **Minimal captions.** Clean sentence-case captions, lower-centre, no animation, no emoji.
9. **Use silence at the peak.** Drop music out for the single most important line (P49).
10. **Linger at the end.** Hold 1–2 s on the final image after the last line.

**Don't:** use SFX hits, punch-ins on every line, meme text, or fast cutting outside the climax.

## Style 3 — Comedy

**Use when:** the source contains a joke, a funny story, banter, an awkward moment, or a genuine laugh; the speaker's delivery is the asset.

**Critical framework sections:** L. Comedic Timing · G. Rhythm · K. Release · E. Retention

**Rules:**
1. **Timing over speed.** The edit serves the punchline — pace changes beat by beat.
2. **Lean setup.** Trim the setup to only what the punchline needs (P56).
3. **Micro-beat before the punch** (0.2–0.5 s) to build anticipation (P57).
4. **Pause after the punchline** — hold 0.5–1.5 s for the laugh to land (P58). Never cut away mid-laugh.
5. **Reaction shot is the second punch.** Cut to the listener's face, a look, or a silence (P59).
6. **Captions withhold the punchline.** The punchline caption appears on the spoken word, never early (P20).
7. **Minimal or no music.** Music kills timing; if used, drop it out entirely before the punchline.
8. **Comedic SFX sparingly** — a record scratch or zoom is allowed once, only if it adds a second laugh.
9. **Rule of three / callbacks** — structure around pattern-pattern-break where the material allows (P60).
10. **End on the laugh** or on the reaction — never on the explanation of the joke.

**Don't:** fill silence after a joke, tighten the speaker's natural rhythm, or add text that explains the joke.

## Style 4 — Educational

**Use when:** the content teaches a framework, explains a concept, gives step-by-step advice, or breaks down data; the viewer's goal is to learn something useful.

**Critical framework sections:** C. Information Hierarchy · A. Purpose · O. Text & Captions · F. Pacing

**Rules:**
1. **Lead with the outcome.** Open with what the viewer will be able to do or understand (Hook 4 or Hook 1).
2. **One concept per segment.** Never introduce two new ideas in the same 5 seconds (P13).
3. **Medium pace.** 3–5 s shots; give each point enough time to be understood, then move.
4. **Concrete before abstract.** Show the example, number, or screen first, then the principle (P14).
5. **Structure is visible.** Numbered on-screen labels ("1/3", "Step 2") for lists and processes.
6. **On-screen text adds information** — definitions, numbers, key terms — never repeats the caption (P74).
7. **B-roll is diagrammatic.** Screen recordings, charts, and demonstrations over decorative footage.
8. **Neutral, low music** (90–110 BPM), no lyrics, ducked well under voice.
9. **Recap at the end.** A one-line summary or the single most actionable takeaway as the final beat.
10. **Saveable.** The ending should make the clip worth saving for later (P80).

**Don't:** cut so fast the point can't be absorbed, use hype SFX, or bury the key takeaway in the middle (P11).

## Style 5 — Inspirational

**Use when:** the content is a turning-point story, a mindset shift, an underdog win, or a rallying message; the target emotion is hope, determination, or pride.

**Critical framework sections:** I. Emotion · K. Release · B. Story · P. Ending & Payoff

**Rules:**
1. **Open on the struggle or the stakes**, not the win (Hook 3 or Hook 5).
2. **Two-phase pacing.** First half: slower, 4–6 s shots, room for the struggle. Second half: accelerate to 2–3 s shots into the peak (P28).
3. **Music builds with the story.** Start sparse, layer in, and hit the full swell exactly on the turning-point line.
4. **One peak line.** Identify the single most powerful sentence; everything builds to it, and it gets clean audio and a held frame (P52).
5. **Brief silence before the peak** to make it land (P49).
6. **Visual lift at the peak** — wider shot, brighter grade, or movement (P54).
7. **Captions gain weight as the clip builds** — keyword emphasis increases toward the peak.
8. **Earned, not manufactured.** The swell only comes after the struggle has been felt (P45).
9. **End within 5 s of the peak** — the last line is the peak line or a short echo of it.
10. **CTA matches the lift.** "Share with someone who needs this" or no CTA at all (P79).

**Don't:** open with the success, stack multiple peaks, or use stock "motivational" footage that doesn't match the story.
