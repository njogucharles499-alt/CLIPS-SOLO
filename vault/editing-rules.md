# 20 Elite Editing Principles

A clip must score ≥16/20 to advance to export. Score each rule Y (pass) or N (fail) during the editing review pass.

---

### Rule 1 — Open on Energy, Not Setup
The first frame must be mid-action, mid-sentence, or mid-emotion. Never open on a speaker settling in, adjusting a mic, or saying "so" or "um". Cut to the moment where energy is already present.

**Fail state:** Clip opens with silence, a breath, or a scene-setting preamble longer than 1 second.

---

### Rule 2 — Kill the Filler
Remove every filler word that does not carry meaning: "um", "uh", "like", "you know", "sort of", "kind of", "basically", "literally" (used as emphasis), "right?". One filler per 30 seconds is the tolerance ceiling.

**Fail state:** More than 2 filler words survive in a 60-second clip.

---

### Rule 3 — Respect the Breath
Do not cut mid-breath or mid-word. Every cut must land on a complete phoneme boundary. Jarring audio edits destroy viewer trust faster than any visual mistake.

**Fail state:** Any audible audio splice or stutter in the exported clip.

---

### Rule 4 — The 3-Second Silence Rule
Silence longer than 3 seconds kills momentum. Either cut the silence or score it with ambient audio. Exception: deliberate dramatic pauses held for effect — these must be ≤2.5 seconds.

**Fail state:** Any unscored silence exceeding 3 seconds.

---

### Rule 5 — One Idea Per Clip
A clip argues exactly one point, tells exactly one story, or delivers exactly one insight. If a transcript segment makes two distinct claims, it is two clips. Combine only when the second claim resolves the first.

**Fail state:** Viewer cannot state the clip's single takeaway in one sentence.

---

### Rule 6 — End Before the Energy Drops
Cut the clip at the emotional or intellectual peak — never after. If the speaker continues after their best line, the clip ends on that line. The last word of the clip should feel like a period, not an ellipsis.

**Fail state:** The clip continues for more than 5 seconds after its peak moment.

---

### Rule 7 — Captions on Every Frame
Burned-in captions must be present on every frame where speech occurs. Zero tolerance for caption gaps. Captions must be readable at mobile size (minimum 36px equivalent, high-contrast).

**Fail state:** Any captionless speech frame, or captions below minimum legibility threshold.

---

### Rule 8 — Caption Accuracy ≥98%
Captions must match spoken words exactly. Auto-generated captions must be reviewed and corrected. Proper nouns, brand names, and numbers are the highest-failure zones — review these manually.

**Fail state:** More than 1 error per 50 words of caption text.

---

### Rule 9 — No Orphan Words
Caption lines must not end with a single orphaned word on the last line (e.g., "I built this company from\nnothing"). Reflow the line break to avoid orphans.

**Fail state:** Any caption block with a single-word final line.

---

### Rule 10 — Audio Peaks in the First 10 Seconds
The highest-energy audio moment in the clip must occur within the first 10 seconds. If the waveform's peak is in the back half, re-evaluate the cut point or restructure the edit.

**Fail state:** The loudest or most energetic audio moment occurs after the 10-second mark.

---

### Rule 11 — Music Serves, Never Competes
Background music must sit at least 12 dB below the speaker's voice. Music that draws the viewer's ear away from the speaker is a distraction, not an enhancement. When in doubt, duck harder.

**Fail state:** Background music audible enough to interfere with speech comprehension.

---

### Rule 12 — No Jump-Cut Fatigue
A jump-cut sequence (multiple cuts within a single continuous take) must not exceed 5 cuts in 10 seconds. Jump-cut saturation destroys retention after the 30-second mark.

**Fail state:** More than 5 visible jump cuts in any 10-second window.

---

### Rule 13 — Colour Grade Consistency
All clips from the same source must share a consistent colour grade. Do not mix colour temperatures (warm vs cool) within a single clip. The speaker's skin tone must be accurate — do not crush it with stylised LUTs.

**Fail state:** Visible colour inconsistency between shots, or inaccurate skin tone rendering.

---

### Rule 14 — Speaker Eyes in the Upper Third
For talking-head clips, the speaker's eyes must sit in the upper third of the frame — not the upper quarter (too tight) and not the middle (too low). Reframe in post if necessary.

**Fail state:** Speaker's eyes above 80% or below 50% of frame height.

---

### Rule 15 — No Dead Zones at Frame Edges
Nothing of visual interest should be cut off by the frame edge. Check left/right/top/bottom edges for cropped text, cut-off hands, or partially visible objects that distract.

**Fail state:** Any visually distracting element within 5% of any frame edge.

---

### Rule 16 — B-Roll Earns Its Place
Every B-roll insert must directly illustrate the spoken word it accompanies. Decorative B-roll that does not reinforce the speaker's claim confuses the viewer. One B-roll clip per one spoken concept.

**Fail state:** Any B-roll insert that does not directly relate to the concurrent voiceover.

---

### Rule 17 — Transitions Are Invisible
Cuts are the only valid transition. Wipes, dissolves, zooms, and template transitions are forbidden unless the clip is explicitly branded as stylised content. The edit is good when the viewer does not notice the cuts.

**Fail state:** Any non-cut transition visible to a viewer not looking for it.

---

### Rule 18 — The 20% Speed Rule
If a clip feels slow, the fix is cutting — not speeding up footage. The only exception: time-lapse sequences of physical action (building, writing, cooking). Sped-up talking-head footage is always visible and always wrong.

**Fail state:** Any talking-head footage running at any speed other than 100%.

---

### Rule 19 — Verify the End-Frame
The final frame before the clip ends must be a clean, expressive image of the speaker — not a blink, not a mouth open mid-word, not a glance away. Trim to the cleanest final frame.

**Fail state:** Final frame shows blink, open mouth, or off-camera glance.

---

### Rule 20 — Export at Source Quality
Never export at a resolution or bitrate lower than the source material. Target: 1080p minimum, 8 Mbps+ for H.264, 4 Mbps+ for H.265. Platform compression will degrade quality; do not pre-degrade.

**Fail state:** Exported file resolution below 1080p or bitrate below platform minimum.
