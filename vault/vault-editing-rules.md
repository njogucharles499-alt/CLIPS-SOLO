# Vault: Elite Editing Rules for Lovable Clips

These are the tactical rules that turn good moments into great clips.

## PACING & CUTS

### Rule 1: Cut On Action or Speech Beat
Don't cut randomly. Cut when:
- Anton finishes a sentence (natural pause)
- He makes a gesture (hand movement peaks)
- Music hits a beat
- Emotion peaks (face tightens, eyes widen)

**Bad:** Cutting mid-word or in dead air
**Good:** Cutting right after "and that's why no-code wins" (full thought delivered)

### Rule 2: Remove Dead Air
Any pause longer than 1 second that doesn't serve emotion = remove it.
- Exception: Silence CAN be powerful if intentional (see Rule 6)
- Default: cut it

### Rule 3: Fast Pacing for Shorts
Short-form means: average shot length 2-4 seconds maximum.
- Hook section: even faster (1-2 second shots)
- Build section: medium (2-3 seconds)
- Peak moment: can hold 3-5 seconds for impact

### Rule 4: Variable Pacing Holds Attention
Don't let cuts stay in a predictable rhythm.
- Fast → slow → fast = keeps viewers guessing
- Predictable rhythm = scrolling past

---

## REACTIONS & AUTHENTICITY

### Rule 5: Hold Reaction Shots
When Anton reacts (laughs, nods, looks surprised):
- Hold the reaction for at least 1 full second after it peaks
- Don't cut away immediately
- Let the viewer absorb the emotion

**In OpusClip:** Mark reactions in your editing script. Don't skip them.

### Rule 6: Silence as Tool
Silence can create:
- Tension (pause before a big reveal)
- Humor (pause after a joke, let it land)
- Weight (pause after he says something important)

**Usage:**
- After a bold claim: 0.5-1 second of silence
- After a joke: 1-2 seconds of silence (let people laugh internally)
- Never more than 2 seconds unless it's a dramatic moment

---

## PROTECTING STUMBLES

### Rule 7: Keep Natural Imperfection
If Anton:
- Laughs mid-sentence
- Says "um" or "uh"
- Pauses to think
- Corrects himself naturally

These are GOOD. They make him human, not robotic.

**Remove only if:**
- The stumble is distracting (multiple stutters in a row)
- It breaks the sentence meaning
- Otherwise: KEEP IT

---

## B-ROLL & VISUALS

### Rule 8: Show Don't Tell
Instead of Anton saying "Lovable is fast," show:
- Code being written in real-time
- Dashboard loading in seconds
- Product shipping screenshots

B-roll should prove what's being said, not just decorate it.

### Rule 9: Match B-Roll to Emotion
If he's describing a problem:
- B-roll is darker, slower, shows frustration
If he's describing Lovable's solution:
- B-roll is bright, fast, shows progress

**Tone matters.** B-roll sets emotion as much as music does.

### Rule 10: Text Overlay Rules
Keep text minimal and on-screen for 2-4 seconds.
- One key phrase per shot
- Large font (readable on phone)
- High contrast (white on dark, or dark on light)
- Position: bottom third to avoid covering faces

**Bad:** "The future of software development is no-code and AI and automation and speed"
**Good:** "No-code" (on one shot) "is the future" (on next shot)

---

## MUSIC & SOUND

### Rule 11: Music Beats Guide Cuts
Cuts on music beats feel intentional.
- Intro beat: establish the topic
- Build beat: intensity increases
- Peak beat: moment of impact
- Out beat: resolution and exit

### Rule 12: Music Energy Matches Message
- Uplifting insight: positive, bright music
- Warning/stakes: tense, driving music
- Celebration/results: energetic, victorious music

### Rule 13: Audio Clarity First
- Dialogue must be crystal clear
- Music supports but doesn't drown it
- Boost Anton's voice slightly above music volume
- No sudden volume jumps

---

## EMOTIONAL MANAGEMENT

### Rule 14: One Emotion Per Clip
Don't jump between anger, humor, hope in a 45-second clip. Pick one primary emotion and layer others subtly.

**Good clip:** Humor as primary, hope as underlying
**Bad clip:** Humor → anger → hope (too many switches)

### Rule 15: Peak Must Land
The clip's most important moment should be:
- Clear and visible
- Held long enough to register (2-3 seconds minimum)
- Supported by music/SFX if needed
- Followed by payoff or silence (not another chaos)

### Rule 16: Ending Should Stick
Last 2-3 seconds are what the viewer remembers.
- End on Anton's face (not black screen)
- End with clarity (not confusion)
- End with takeaway (not more questions)
- Option: end with a text quote of his key phrase

---

## PLATFORM-SPECIFIC

### Rule 17: Vertical Composition (9:16)
- Keep faces centered in top 2/3 of frame
- Text stays in bottom third (doesn't cover faces)
- No important details on far edges (will be cropped on phones)
- Wide shots are risky; medium close-ups are safer

### Rule 18: Short Window Assumption
User is scrolling fast. First 2 seconds must grab or they're gone.
- Make the hook VISUAL as well as verbal
- Use text on-screen to announce the topic
- Zoom in, brighten, or add subtle SFX to grab attention

---

## CONTINUITY & FLOW

### Rule 19: Logical Sequence
One idea should lead naturally to the next.
- If he says "problem," next shot shows the problem
- If he says "solution," next shot shows the solution
- Don't jump ideas without transition

### Rule 20: Consistency in Style
- Same font style for all captions
- Same music "feel" throughout (don't switch genres mid-clip)
- Color grading consistent (don't jump from warm to cold)
- Reaction shots always use same framing angle

---

## QUALITY CHECKS

### Pre-Export Checklist
- [ ] Dialogue is clear and audible
- [ ] No jarring volume jumps
- [ ] No glitches, black frames, or technical errors
- [ ] All captions spell correctly and match the speech
- [ ] Transitions feel intentional, not random
- [ ] The clip works on a small phone screen (not just 1080p monitor)
- [ ] First 3 seconds hook the viewer
- [ ] Last 3 seconds are memorable
- [ ] Total duration is within target (30-90s range)
- [ ] #LovablePartner is in caption template (not on-screen)

---

## The Fundamental Rule

Every edit must earn its place. If removing a cut makes the clip better, remove it. Elite editing is the controlled elimination of waste.

Ask: "Does this serve the message, emotion, or retention?" If no → cut it.
